To examine whether metadata documentation quality relates to cross-source score agreement, we analyzed the 8 independent collision pairs identified in Tables~5 and~6, computing a per-pair metadata completeness score (sum of documented fields across harness, n-shot, chain-of-thought, temperature, and prompt template; range 0--5) and the absolute score divergence $|\Delta|$ between the two sources. Completeness varied from 0 (no fields documented) to 2 (harness and n-shot documented), while $|\Delta|$ ranged from 0.07 to 32.33 percentage points. A Spearman rank correlation yielded $\rho = -0.871$ ($p = 0.005$, $n = 8$), suggesting a negative association between completeness and score divergence---i.e., pairs with more complete metadata documentation tended to exhibit smaller cross-source discrepancies. A directional analysis restricted to the five Tulu-3-8B pairs (where OLv2 serves as a third-party source and the HF Model Card as a first-party source) revealed a mean first-party advantage of -0.032 points (range: [-0.23, +0.19]), with 2 of 5 pairs showing higher first-party scores, though the inconsistent direction across benchmarks and the small magnitude caution against strong directional claims. Given the small sample ($n = 8$), these results should be treated as illustrative evidence of a plausible relationship between documentation practices and reproducibility, rather than as definitive proof of a causal link.
